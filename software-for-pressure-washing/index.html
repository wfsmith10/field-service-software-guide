Scheduling and invoicing software for pressure-washing businesses: what a seasonal year costs

Edited by William Smith · researched with AI assistance · Last updated 2026-11-27 · How we make pages

Short answer: if you wash for only part of the year, the cheapest way to pay depends on how many months you work. We used each vendor's own pricing page (checked 2026-11-27) and counted only the subscription. For a solo operator, paying monthly only in the months you work is cheaper than prepaying a year if you work up to 7 months on Jobber, up to 8 on Housecall Pro, up to 10 on QuoteIQ (by 9 cents), and up to 11 on Markate. Work more months than that and the annual price wins. Of the five, only ResponsiBid, a quoting tool that works alongside a CRM, publishes a seasonal pause price ($50 a month for up to 4 months). Jobber's terms say a paid "hibernation" may be offered at its discretion, with no published price. Housecall Pro says billing can be paused only after a phone call with the account owner.

Pressure-washing owners compare these tools mostly on price. One example is "Honest pricing comparison between Jobber…" on r/PressureWashingGrowth. The top "best pressure washing software" lists we read on 2026-11-27 give the monthly price but do not work out the cost of a part-year season. This page adds the part a seasonal business pays for: the year. We have not used these products. Every price and feature below comes from the vendors' own pricing pages, product pages, help articles and terms of service.

  • Jobber's middle option does not lower a seasonal washer's cost. Monthly with a 1-year commitment is $39 × 12 = $468 a year. That is $120 more than the annual price, and Jobber's FAQ says that if you cancel during the year, "you'll continue to be billed monthly until the end of the 12-month term." It spreads the cost over the year. It does not lower it.
  • QuoteIQ's annual price is 10 monthly payments. Its pricing page says "You pay for 10 months and get 12 months of service." So for a 10-month season, the two options cost almost the same.
  • Markate's annual saving is small ("save 8%"), so month to month stays cheaper unless you work all 12 months.

Before you cancel for the winter: pause rules and your data

Paying monthly only in season works only if cancelling and coming back is painless. Here is what each vendor publishes:

  • Jobber. Its terms of service (updated September 22, 2026) say users "may, in Jobber’s discretion, have the option to enter a state of account hibernation for a nominal fee". Access "may be restricted or limited." No price or length is published. Its cancel help article says that after your paid period ends "you'll lose access to your account data," and suggests contacting Support to pause. An older Jobber help article (updated April 2024) says a cancelled account "still exists," so you could reactivate it, and names seasonal work as a reason to cancel rather than permanently close.
  • Housecall Pro. You cancel through the support chat. The help center says, "we cannot fully process your cancellation or pause billing until a team member has spoken with the account owner over the phone." Its terms say fees "will not be prorated or refunded" and that a cancelled account "will be disabled within 14 days." So an annual plan cancelled in October does not give back the winter months.
  • QuoteIQ. No pause is mentioned on its pricing page. You can cancel from account settings with "no contracts or cancellation fees", and "Your data remains available for export." Its price lock applies only while you stay on the same plan. If you cancel and later return, QuoteIQ tells you to confirm the price rather than assume an old offer carries over.
  • Markate. No pause is mentioned on its pricing page. You cancel by contacting support or letting the payment lapse. There are no refunds within a billing period. It recommends downloading your data as CSV or PDF before cancelling.
  • ResponsiBid. The only one with a published seasonal price: $50 a month for at most 4 months per calendar year, on monthly plans. While paused, your website quote form can stay up but you "lose all functionality for editing bids," and automations keep running. Example on Scaling: 8 months × $179 + 4 months × $50 = $1,632, compared with 12 × $179 = $2,148.

How to choose for a seasonal washing business

  1. Count your paying months honestly. Use the table above to see whether month to month or annual is cheaper for that number.
  2. If you choose month to month, plan the off-season exit. Export your client list before you cancel, and check what each vendor keeps. Jobber and Housecall Pro say to contact support about pausing.
  3. If quoting eats your evenings in spring, compare a quoting add-on (ResponsiBid, with a published pause price) against QuoteIQ's built-in measuring and InstaQuote tiers.
  4. If you also do other trades, see Housecall Pro alternatives by crew size and Jobber's price history. Prices have changed before.
