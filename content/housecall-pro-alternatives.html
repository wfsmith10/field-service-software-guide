Short answer: on the vendors' own pricing pages (checked 2026-11-25), Kickserv and Markate cost less than Housecall Pro at 5 and 10 users, and Jobber costs less at 1 and 10 users. Jobber matches Housecall Pro at 5 users ($149/mo billed annually). ServiceM8 does not charge per user at all. It charges by jobs per month, from $29 to $349. Workiz and FieldPulse do not publish prices. A lower subscription is not the whole bill, though. Kickserv's online card rate (3.9% + 30¢) is higher than the others listed, so on $10,000 a month of card payments it can cost more in total than Housecall Pro. The numbers are below, with the arithmetic shown.

Owners often look for a replacement right after a price change. One example is "House Call Pro raised prices on me again" on r/WhichCRM. This page puts Housecall Pro's old and current prices next to five alternatives that publish theirs, for crews of 1, 5 and 10. We have not used these products. Every price comes from the vendors' public pricing pages and help articles, or from dated copies saved by the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine.

Worked example (our arithmetic): a 5-person crew collects $10,000 a month by card over 40 online payments, billed annually.

  • Housecall Pro: $149 + at least 2.59% × $10,000 ($259) = at least $408 a month. That is the lowest advertised rate. The page does not say who gets it.
  • Jobber: $149 + 2.9% × $10,000 ($290) + 40 × $0.30 ($12) = $451 a month.
  • Kickserv: $60 + 3.9% × $10,000 ($390) + 40 × $0.30 ($12) = $462 a month. Kickserv's "Kickback" takes 5% off the subscription if you process at least $1,000 a month on Start, which is $3 here. Kickserv also lets you add a fee to card-paid invoices.

So the cheapest subscription can be the most expensive total if most customers pay online by card. If they pay by bank transfer or tap a card in person, the subscription matters more. Ask Markate, ServiceM8 and Joist for their card rates before you compare.

What you must move, and how

Taken from each vendor's own help articles (checked 2026-11-25). Do this before your Housecall Pro renewal date.

  1. Export from Housecall Pro while you still have access. Only Admins can export. Customers: Customers → Actions → Export. Jobs: Jobs → Actions → Export. Housecall Pro emails each list as a CSV file from notifications@housecallpro.com. The help article covers the customer and job lists only. For the price book it links a separate article, "Price Book: Import or Export Services and Materials." It does not describe exporting photos or attachments, so ask support how to get those out.
  2. Check the file before you load it. Count the rows against your customer count, and open a few customers you know well.
  3. Moving to Jobber: client import takes CSV, TSV or PSV files. Only admin users can import. Each file must be under 2.5MB, which Jobber puts at about 5,000 rows, so split bigger lists. Custom lead sources do not import; only Facebook, Google, Instagram, Flyer, Referral, Vehicle Wrap and Other do. The import will not run in a private or incognito browser window. If your clients are already in QuickBooks, Jobber suggests syncing QuickBooks first to avoid duplicates. Jobber's help center lists separate import articles for quotes, jobs and invoices.
  4. Moving to ServiceM8: Clients → Bulk Import. Download ServiceM8's template and rename your columns to match its headings. If you connect Xero, QuickBooks Online or MYOB, ServiceM8 says your clients and items come in automatically, so you may not need the CSV.
  5. Moving to Kickserv: the cloud icon with an up arrow imports contacts from a CSV file or a Gmail account. If you connect QuickBooks, Kickserv brings your customers over automatically.
  6. Moving to Markate: its pricing page lists data migration as included. It says its team moves "customer information, job records, and financial data" in 1-3 business days.
  7. List the settings that are not in your customer and job lists. Examples are automated texts and emails, review requests, online booking links and recurring service plans. None of the import articles above mention moving them, so plan to set them up again in the new tool before you cancel.

Checklist before you switch

  1. Read your last two Housecall Pro invoices. Find which line went up: plan, users, add-ons or a promotion ending.
  2. Count everyone who logs in, office staff included. For ServiceM8, count new jobs per month instead.
  3. Price the plan that has the features you use today, not just the entry plan. For example, Kickserv's dispatch map starts on Run, and Jobber's QuickBooks sync starts on Connect.
  4. Add card fees using your own card volume and number of payments.
  5. Compare annual and monthly totals. Annual billing is cheaper, but Kickserv says annual plans are not refundable.
  6. Use the free trial with your real jobs, then check every pricing page again on the day you buy. Prices change.
